English grammar · meaning changes

Verb + to do
or verb + doing?

Some verbs can take either form, but the meaning changes. Think of the infinitive as a next step, intention, or action not yet done; the -ing form often looks back at an experience, activity, or action itself.

verb + to + infinitiveoften: intention · next action · something to do
≠
verb + -ingoften: experience · activity · action as a fact

forget memory
forget + to doFail to remember that you must do it.
I forgot to call Mr Jones.
forget + doingNot remember a past experience.
I’ll never forget driving in that snowstorm.
remember memory
remember + to doRemember that you need to do it.
Chris always remembers to lock the door.
remember + doingRecall doing it in the past.
He didn’t remember lending me his pen.
mean intention / result
mean + to doIntend or plan to do something.
George meant to check your report.
mean + doingInvolve or result in something.
Growing up means having new responsibilities.
regret feeling
regret + to doFeel sorry about what you are about to say/do. Often formal.
We regret to inform you your flight is delayed.
regret + doingFeel sorry about something already done.
He has never regretted turning down the promotion.
try effort / experiment
try + to doMake an effort; attempt something difficult.
She tried to carry her suitcase upstairs.
try + doingTest a method as an experiment.
Try making a list so you don’t forget.
stop pause / quit
stop + to doPause one activity in order to do another.
We stopped to admire the view.
stop + doingQuit or cease the activity.
You should stop eating sweets.
go on next / continue
go on + to doFinish one thing, then begin the next.
He went on to answer questions.
go on + doingContinue the same activity.
The student went on writing.
hate general / specific
hate + to doBe sorry or reluctant to do something (often a specific occasion).
I hate to tell you this…
hate + doingDislike an activity in general.
John hates waking up early.
Quick memory hook
to do → forward: a task, intention, or next step.   doing → look back / at the activity: a memory, experience, or thing you stop/continue/enjoy.
These are useful patterns, not an absolute rule. The exact contrast depends on the verb; learn each pair with its example.
